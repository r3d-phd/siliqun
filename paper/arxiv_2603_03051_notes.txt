Paper: arXiv:2603.03051
Title: Scaling of silicon spin qubits under correlated noise
Authors: Juan S. Rojas-Arias, Leon C. Camenzind, Yi-Hsien Wu, Peter Stano, Akito Noiri, Kenta Takeda, Takashi Nakajima, Takashi Kobayashi, Giordano Scappucci, Daniel Loss, Seigo Tarucha
Submitted: March 3, 2026
26 pages, 5 main figures, 10 Extended Data figures
Subjects: cond-mat.mes-hall, quant-ph

ABSTRACT KEY POINTS:
1. Focuses on scaling silicon spin qubits while remaining compatible with QEC
2. Silicon spin qubits: industrial fabrication compatible, nanoscale footprint, millions of qubits on a chip
3. Problem: spatially correlated noise from close qubit proximity erodes QEC redundancy
4. Experiment: quantify spatial extent of noise correlations in a FIVE-QUBIT silicon array
5. Two distinct correlated noise sources identified:
   a) Global magnetic field drifts → perfectly correlated fluctuations (critical, but mitigable)
   b) Charge noise from two-level fluctuators → short-range correlations decaying within neighboring qubits
6. Magnetic drifts can compromise QEC but can be mitigated
7. Charge noise correlations are moderate, electrically tunable, compatible with fault-tolerant operation with minimal qubit overhead
8. Establishes quantitative benchmarks for correlated noise in scalable qubit arrays

RELEVANCE TO OUR WORK:
- Directly relevant to SiliQun: we simulate silicon spin qubits with DFS encoding
- DFS encoding specifically protects against CORRELATED noise (collective dephasing)
- This paper experimentally validates the noise model we simulate
- The charge noise correlations they measure could inform our noise parameters
- Their 5-qubit array is exactly the scale we benchmark (though we go to 25 encoded qubits)
