\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{SiliQun}, an open-source, physics-grounded simulation platform
that unifies three silicon spin qubit hardware technologies---silicon
metal-oxide-semiconductor (SiMOS) quantum dots, phosphorus-donor electron spin
qubits, and gate-all-around (GAA) nanowire spin qubits---within a single
Lindblad density-matrix physics engine and a Gymnasium-compatible reinforcement
learning interface.
SiliQun's Software-Defined Quantum Firmware (SDQF) architecture separates the
physics engine from the hardware profile, enabling the same RL agent to be
trained and evaluated across all three technologies by changing a single
constructor argument.
Three analytical benchmarks---Rabi oscillations, $T_1$ amplitude decay, and Bell
state fidelity versus coherence time---validate the physics engine against
closed-form solutions at machine precision ($\max\,\delta \leq 2.78 \times
10^{-16}$).
We validate SiliQun through four independent experiments spanning three hardware
technologies and four RL algorithms.
Replication~1 reproduces the deep Q-learning and SARSA CZ gate-synthesis results
of Daraeizadeh~et~al.\ on SiliQun's SiMOS profile, achieving mean fidelity
$\bar{F} = 0.9996 \pm 0.0001$ and $0.9994 \pm 0.0002$ respectively, within
$0.04\%$ of the published values.
Replication~2 reproduces the DQN universal state-preparation results of
He~et~al.\ on SiliQun's donor-electron profile, achieving a mean cardinal-state
fidelity of $\bar{F} = 0.9748 \pm 0.0098$.
Extension~3 applies the same DQN protocol to SiliQun's GAA profile,
establishing the first machine-learning benchmark for GAA silicon spin qubits
with mean fidelity $\bar{F} = 0.9877 \pm 0.0021$.
A fourth experiment using Proximal Policy Optimisation on the SiMOS profile
achieves $F = 1.000$ across all five independent seeds, converging within
200 episodes and demonstrating that SiliQun supports modern policy-gradient
algorithms without modification.
Across all four experiments, consistent cross-technology fidelity confirms that
SiliQun's physics engine correctly models hardware-specific decoherence rather
than introducing simulator-induced artefacts.
SiliQun is released under the MIT licence and is fully reproducible from the
provided scripts and Aziz HPC job files.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The Simulation Gap in Silicon Spin Qubit Control}
\label{subsec:simulation_gap}

Silicon metal-oxide-semiconductor (SiMOS) spin qubits have emerged as a leading
candidate for scalable quantum computing, offering coherence times approaching
$T_1 \sim 1$\,ms and $T_2 \sim 0.5$\,ms in recent demonstrations~\cite{Xue2022,
Noiri2022,Philips2022,Steinacker2023}, compatibility with standard CMOS
fabrication, and a nearest-neighbour qubit topology that maps naturally onto
one-dimensional quantum circuit architectures.
Yet the very precision that makes SiMOS qubits attractive also makes their
control exceptionally sensitive to noise.
Charge noise, hyperfine coupling fluctuations, and residual spin-orbit
interaction degrade gate fidelity in ways that are difficult to anticipate from
first principles alone~\cite{Veldhorst2015,Fogarty2018}.

Developing noise-aware gate synthesis policies for SiMOS devices requires a
simulation environment that is simultaneously physically accurate and compatible
with standard machine learning toolchains.
Existing frameworks satisfy one condition but not both: Lindblad solvers such as
QuTiP~\cite{Lambert2024} provide accurate open-system dynamics but offer no
reinforcement learning interface; RL environments for quantum circuit synthesis
use abstract depolarising noise rather than first-principles device
models~\cite{VanDerLinde2023,QiskitGym2024}.
This gap between physical accuracy and algorithmic accessibility is the central
problem that SiliQun addresses.

\subsection{The Physics-Grounded RL Simulation Methodology}
\label{subsec:intro_pgirs}

We propose the \textbf{Physics-Grounded RL Simulation} (PGIRS) methodology as
a principled approach to building simulation platforms that are both physically
accurate and algorithmically accessible.
PGIRS is organised around five design principles:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Noise-First Design:} The noise model is the primary design
    constraint. All simulator components are validated against the noise
    environment before the RL interface is constructed.
  \item \textbf{Gymnasium Compliance:} The simulator exposes a fully compliant
    Gymnasium \texttt{Env} interface, ensuring compatibility with any standard
    RL library without modification.
  \item \textbf{Reward Engineering:} The reward function is treated as a
    first-class design concern, with weights discovered automatically via
    Bayesian optimisation rather than hand-tuned.
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical Decomposition:} Complex multi-qubit objectives are
    decomposed into sequences of sub-goals, each providing a dense intermediate
    reward signal.
  \item \textbf{Empirical Validation:} Every simulator component is validated
    against analytical benchmarks, cross-algorithm comparisons, and real
    hardware transfer experiments.
\end{itemize}

SiliQun is the reference implementation of PGIRS for the SiMOS device family.
While PGIRS addresses the methodological gaps in physics-grounded RL simulation, current tools face concrete implementation barriers that prevent its adoption.
\subsection{Limitations of Existing Frameworks}
\label{subsec:intro_limitations}

No existing open-source framework satisfies both conditions required for
physics-grounded RL simulation of SiMOS devices: physical accuracy and
a standard RL interface.
QuTiP~\cite{Lambert2024} provides a comprehensive Lindblad solver but offers
no Gymnasium interface and is not designed for RL training loops.
Qiskit Pulse~\cite{Alexander2020} provides pulse-level control for IBM
superconducting hardware but has no SiMOS device model.
C3~\cite{Wittler2021} supports closed-loop optimal control via GRAPE and
CMA-ES but does not implement standard RL algorithms.
TensorFlow Quantum~\cite{Broughton2020} supports hybrid quantum-classical ML
via Cirq integration but targets gate-model circuits rather than device-level
spin qubit physics.
Gymnasium-compatible RL environments for quantum circuit synthesis (qgym~\cite{
VanDerLinde2023}, Qiskit Gym~\cite{QiskitGym2024}) use abstract depolarising
noise rather than first-principles Lindblad dynamics, making them unsuitable
for SiMOS-specific policy training.
The Oxford DPhil work of Orbell~\cite{Orbell2024} addresses SiMOS device
tuning and calibration via Bayesian optimisation and pulse-level optimal
control, but operates at the device characterisation layer rather than the
gate synthesis layer; it provides no open-source Gymnasium environment.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{subsec:contributions}

This paper makes three categories of contribution, ordered by their centrality
to the SiliQun simulation platform.

\textbf{Primary contributions --- the SiliQun simulation platform:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{SiliQun physics engine:} A Lindblad density-matrix simulator
    calibrated to the SiMOS parameter regime, validated against three
    analytical benchmarks at machine precision ($\max\,\delta \leq 2.78 \times
    10^{-16}$), with a 93$\times$ GPU speedup enabling practical RL training
    throughput.

  \item \textbf{Multi-technology hardware profiles:} A \texttt{TechnologyProfile}
    abstraction encoding the complete physics characterisation of SiMOS,
    donor-electron, Gate-All-Around (GAA), GaAs singlet-triplet, and SLEDGE
    silicon spin qubit technologies, each grounded in published calibration data
    and switchable by a single constructor argument.

  \item \textbf{Gymnasium-compatible interface:} A fully compliant
    \texttt{SiliQunEnv} environment with a discrete 11-token gate action
    space, a 16-element density-matrix state representation, and a
    four-component reward function with automatically discovered weights.

  \item \textbf{Qiskit BackendV2 provider:} A pip-installable package
    (\texttt{siliqun-provider}) that registers SiliQun as a Qiskit
    \texttt{BackendV2}-compatible backend, enabling immediate integration
    with the Qiskit ecosystem and standard \texttt{transpile()} workflows.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Secondary contribution --- the PGIRS engineering methodology:}
\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{4}
  \item \textbf{PGIRS methodology:} A five-phase engineering discipline
    (Noise-First Design, Gymnasium Compliance, Reward Engineering,
    Hierarchical Decomposition, Empirical Validation) that governs the
    design, implementation, and validation of physics-grounded RL
    simulation platforms. SiliQun is the reference implementation of PGIRS
    for the silicon spin qubit domain.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Tertiary contributions --- empirical validation of the platform:}
\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{5}
  \item \textbf{Cross-algorithm benchmark:} A systematic evaluation of six DRL
    algorithm families (DQL, SARSA, PPO, DQN, TQC, SAC) on the 4-qubit GHZ
    synthesis task under SiMOS noise, providing the first comparative benchmark
    of DRL algorithms on a physically calibrated silicon spin qubit environment.

  \item \textbf{Cross-backend validation:} A multi-backend dispatch interface
    transferring SiliQun-trained circuits across four backends (SiliQun,
    Qiskit Aer, FakeSherbrooke, and GaAs singlet-triplet). TQC achieves
    $\bar{F} = 0.9036 \pm 0.0914$ on direct GaAs CNOT synthesis, with two of
    four seeds exceeding $F \geq 0.986$, confirming hardware-portable policy
    transfer across silicon spin qubit architectures.

  \item \textbf{GAA device generalisation:} Validation on a Gate-All-Around
    silicon device profile demonstrating that SiliQun's device-profile
    abstraction transfers across silicon qubit architectures without retraining,
    achieving $\bar{F} = 0.994 \pm 0.012$ on 2-qubit Bell synthesis across
    five seeds.
\end{enumerate}
